Just Energy Transition — Bridging Communities and Institutions

2025 MSc MADE Energie Lab Zuidoost Stichting EGK

How can an energy transition be just if the people most affected struggle to be heard?

During my MSc MADE, I worked in a team of five with Energie Lab Zuidoost to explore this question in Amsterdam Zuidoost. While the energy transition is often discussed in terms of technology, infrastructure and policy, our research focused on something less tangible: the relationships between residents, community organisations and institutions.

Through fieldwork and interviews, we learned how difficult it can be for residents to navigate institutional systems and access available support. We also encountered people and organisations already doing the work of connecting these different worlds. These bridge builders had established relationships and trust within their communities, yet their role was not always recognised or supported by institutions.

Building on these insights, we organised three co-creation sessions with community bridge builders and institutional stakeholders. Rather than immediately searching for solutions, we created space to discuss the challenges they experienced in their work. Conversations touched on institutional accountability, access to subsidies and fragmented relationships between local initiatives.

What stood out to me was how important it was to create a setting where these tensions could be discussed openly, without the municipality directing the conversation.

The co-creation process was not about finding a quick technical solution. It was about making relationships and tensions visible, and creating opportunities for people from different positions to understand one another.

Alongside the research and facilitation, I took a leading role in translating our findings into visual material. I developed a stakeholder network map to make the relationships between community organisations, bridge builders and institutions more visible, and contributed to an explanatory video and project website to communicate our process and recommendations.

This project made me realise that a just energy transition is not only about providing resources or inviting people to participate. It also requires recognising existing community knowledge, investing in relationships and creating conditions in which different perspectives can genuinely influence decisions.
