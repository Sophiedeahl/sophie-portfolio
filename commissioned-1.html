Mapping Mobility: Identifying Opportunities for Neighbourhood Hubs

2021 Collaboration with Advier & SVP Spatial Analysis / Urban Research

Where do mobility hubs make sense?

As part of a project by Advier and SVP for the Municipality of Utrecht, I explored this question by mapping the relationships between public transport, shared mobility services, cycling and walking accessibility, and important destinations across the city.

I used Google My Maps, printed maps and hand-drawn analysis to bring together different layers of mobility information. At this early stage, the aim was not to calculate a definitive ranking of locations, but to explore how existing services, accessibility and urban destinations related to one another. The maps helped make these relationships visible and provided a starting point for discussing potential mobility hub locations with the project team.

What I liked about this project was the challenge of making sense of a city through information that was scattered across different sources. It was part detective work, part spatial puzzle: figuring out which connections were relevant, where opportunities might exist and how to make those findings understandable to others. It's the kind of work I enjoy most, somewhere between research, visual thinking and practical urban questions.

My spatial analysis contributed to the team's exploration of possible pilot locations and formed part of a broader advisory report for the Municipality of Utrecht.
