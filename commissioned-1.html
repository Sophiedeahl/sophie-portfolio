Mapping Mobility: Identifying Opportunities for Neighbourhood Hubs

2021 Collaboration with Advier & SVP

Where should a neighbourhood mobility hub be located to actually improve how people move through a city?

As part of a project by Advier and SVP for the Municipality of Utrecht, I explored this question by mapping the relationships between public transport, shared mobility services, cycling and walking accessibility, and important destinations across the city.

I used Google My Maps, printed maps and hand-drawn analysis to bring together different layers of mobility information. At this early stage, the aim was not to calculate a definitive ranking of locations, but to explore how existing services, accessibility and urban destinations related to one another. The maps helped make these relationships visible and provided a starting point for discussing potential mobility hub locations with the project team.

What I find interesting about this project is that the location of a mobility hub is not just a spatial decision, but a question of how people experience and access the city. A map can reveal where services overlap, but it also raises questions about whose journeys are being supported and whose needs might be overlooked.

Comparing the different mapping layers revealed concentrations of mobility services and important urban destinations across Utrecht. These patterns helped identify areas worth exploring as potential locations for neighbourhood mobility hubs.

My spatial analysis contributed to the team's evaluation of possible pilot locations, alongside additional spatial and accessibility criteria.

The research formed part of a broader advisory report for the Municipality of Utrecht.
