BIOGRAPHY

Calling myself a researcher, designer and visualiser might sound a bit vague, but I do think it is the best description of how I see myself.

In 2015 I finished Industrial Engineering, also called Product Design. This four year programme prepared me to work as an engineer. I learned 3D modelling, product development, drawing, technical drawing, entrepreneurial skills and more. I learned to bring my ideas to life, from sketch to finished product. I also spent six months in Zanzibar, helping set up a workshop where local women make products from local waste. That experience showed me the world is bigger than the Netherlands, or Europe alone, and that people do things very differently in different places. For my final internship and graduation project at TomTom, I designed a small alarm device for women, as part of their push to design for women.

After graduating I was aiming for a product engineering job. Instead I went back to my old job, modelling, which I had done since I was 18. I thought it would be for a year or so, but it turned into five years. Only when Covid stopped me from travelling did I look for a next step. I ended up somewhere a bit out of the box: urban development. I worked on projects that researched urban challenges and came up with solutions in the form of design and consultancy. The projects ranged from mobility and urban planning to policy, governance and wider environmental challenges, such as sustainable energy.

These topics made me want to go deeper. I had never studied the urban environment in an academic setting. So I did a master's in Metropolitan Analysis, Design and Engineering, a joint degree at TU Delft and Wageningen University and Research. It covers the city through many topics: climate, energy, water, justice, policy, technology, AI and data, ethics and ecology. I learned to do proper research and data analysis. I learned to code in HTML, Python and Blueprint. All assignments were done in multidisciplinary teams with students from different universities, so I became a good team player. We also focused a lot on qualitative research, and we ran numerous participatory design sessions with citizens. My thesis looked at collaboration between the stakeholders who make decisions in urban densification. That added knowledge of collaborative governance to my toolkit.

In both of my studies I found that I was strong at visualising ideas, whether through drawing, video, infographics or presentations. I find this an important and often overlooked part of engineering and design. That is true in product design and urban design, and most of all in research.

Now I am ready for my next step in London. I am half British, and I thought: why not try another city, a big one?

It might not be completely clear which field I belong to, but I am interested in both equally. I would be happy to work in product design or urban design, on the practical side, the research side, or both. Most of all, I want to work on projects that make our living environment better for people now and in the future, with respect for everyone and everything that lives in it.
