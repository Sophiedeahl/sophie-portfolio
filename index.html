BIOGRAPHY

Calling myself a researcher, designer and visualiser might sound a bit vague, but I do think it is the best description of how I see myself.

In 2015 I finished Industrial Engineering, also called Product Design. I learned to bring my ideas to life, from sketch to finished product. I spent six months in Zanzibar, helping set up a workshop where local women make products from local waste. It showed me that the world is bigger than the Netherlands, and that people do things very differently in different places. For my graduation project at TomTom, I designed a small alarm device for women.

I then went back to modelling, which I had done since I was 18. I thought it would be for a year, but it turned into five. When Covid stopped me from travelling, I ended up somewhere a bit out of the box: urban development. I worked on mobility, policy, governance and energy. That made me want to go deeper, so I did a master's in Metropolitan Analysis, Design and Engineering at TU Delft and Wageningen University. I learned to code, to do qualitative research, and to run participatory design sessions with citizens. In both studies I found that I am strong at visualising ideas.

Now I am ready for my next step in London. I am half British, and I thought: why not try another city, a big one? I would be happy to work in product design or urban design, on the practical side or the research side. Most of all, I want to work on projects that make our living environment better for people now and in the future, with respect for everyone and everything that lives in it.
